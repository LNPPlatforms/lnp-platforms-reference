The problem

The ear exam is the least objective test in the room.

Fever and ear pain don't confirm an infection. Earwax and poor lighting hide the eardrum. A crying child's eardrum turns red. Providers do their best with what they can see, and the research shows how often that falls short.3

90% / 70%

Providers felt highly certain in 90% of ear infection diagnoses. Only 70% were true infections.4

26%

of antibiotic prescriptions for ear infections in that study were potentially unnecessary.4

57%

Even four ENT specialists agreed on the diagnosis in only 57% of cases. Adding a tympanometry reading raised their agreement and certainty.6

What the specialty guidance says. The American Academy of Pediatrics holds that otitis media should not be diagnosed when the tympanogram is normal, and the American Academy of Otolaryngology recommends tympanometry whenever there is doubt about middle ear fluid.8
Who it protects

Two patients. Two mistakes a tympanogram prevents.

🧒

Kids

Ear infections are the most common reason children get antibiotics.5 Two things go wrong:

  • Overdiagnosis. A red eardrum from crying gets treated as an infection, and the child gets an antibiotic they didn't need.
  • Missed fluid. Middle ear fluid is very common and easy to miss. Left alone, it can lead to speech, language and developmental delays.
🧑

Adults

Most adult "ear infections" are misdiagnosed. The one that matters most is easy to miss:

  • Sudden nerve hearing loss looks like fluid behind the eardrum, but it isn't.
  • Patients have about a two-week window to see an ENT for a steroid injection that can help preserve their hearing.8
  • A normal tympanogram rules out fluid, points to nerve hearing loss and triggers an urgent ENT referral.
How it works

Ten seconds. One clear answer.

Seal the probe

The handheld easyTymp rests gently in the ear canal. No pain and no prep, so it works on a squirming toddler.

Read the curve

In about 10 seconds it measures eardrum movement and middle ear pressure. A peak means no fluid. Flat means fluid.

Treat and bill

Your provider treats with confidence, documents the result and bills under the dedicated tympanometry CPT code (92567).

It also takes away the "red ear" reason to prescribe antibiotics during viral colds, which supports antibiotic stewardship and your HEDIS quality scores.
